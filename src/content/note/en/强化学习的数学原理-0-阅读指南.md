This series follows Professor Shiyu Zhao's *Mathematical Foundations of Reinforcement Learning*. The English articles are the main notes; the Chinese pages provide summaries and reading links. Chapters 1–10 now form a continuous path from modeling an MDP to value approximation, policy gradients, and actor–critic learning.

The first three chapters were revised on 27 September 2026. This revision corrects the Markov-property formulas, transition-matrix conventions, optimality proofs, and conditions for reward transformations. It also replaces the earlier external screenshot examples with one fully specified example and adds runnable experiments.

## The example and notation

Our 2×2 Gridworld has states $A,B,C,T$, arranged as top row $(A,B)$ and bottom row $(C,T)$. Actions are right and down; moving outside the grid leaves the state unchanged. Entering $T$ gives reward 1, all other transitions give 0, and $T$ is absorbing with zero subsequent reward. The discount is 0.9. The uniform policy selects each action with probability 1/2.

The exact reference values are

$$
v_{\text{uniform}}=(9/11,10/11,10/11,0)^T,
\qquad v^*=(0.9,1,1,0)^T.
$$

| Notation | Convention |
| --- | --- |
| $S_t,A_t,R_{t+1},S_{t+1}$ | Current state, chosen action, resulting reward and next state |
| $p(s',r\mid s,a)$ | Joint environment model; $\bar r(s,a)$ is expected immediate reward |
| $G_t$ | $\sum_{k\ge0}\gamma^kR_{t+k+1}$ |
| $v_\pi,q_\pi$ | True values for a fixed policy |
| $V,Q$ or $v_k$ | Estimated values or iterates, depending on context |
| $q_v$ | One-step action lookahead from an arbitrary vector $v$ |
| $P_{\pi,ij}$ | Probability of moving from state $i$ to state $j$ |
| $T_\pi,T_*$ | Policy-evaluation and optimality operators |

## Math toolbox

You need probability distributions, conditional expectation, matrix multiplication, geometric series, and basic differentiation. The less familiar tools are introduced where they are used:

- **Total expectation:** $\mathbb E[X]=\sum_y\Pr(Y=y)\mathbb E[X\mid Y=y]$. In Chapter 2 it decomposes return by action and next outcome.
- **Conditional expectation:** a mean given available information. Chapter 6 conditions on the entire observed past to state the noise assumptions correctly.
- **Infinity norm:** $\|v\|_\infty=\max_i|v_i|$. A row-stochastic matrix satisfies $\|Pv\|_\infty\le\|v\|_\infty$.
- **Neumann series:** Chapter 2 proves that $(I-\gamma P)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}\gamma^kP^k$ for the discounted stochastic matrices used here.
- **Contraction mapping:** Chapter 3 proves the fixed-point theorem and then checks its hypotheses for the Bellman operator.
- **Weighted projection:** Chapter 8 derives the least-squares projection and the projected Bellman fixed point, keeping the training-state weights explicit.
- **Log derivative:** $\nabla\pi=\pi\nabla\log\pi$ turns a policy derivative into a sampleable score; Chapter 9 derives the identity and baseline cancellation.
- **Importance sampling:** Chapter 10 proves $\mathbb E_\beta[(\pi/\beta)f]=\mathbb E_\pi[f]$ at a fixed state and separately treats state weighting.
- **Almost-sure convergence:** the event that the sample sequence converges has probability one. It is stronger than seeing a downward trend in an averaged plot; Chapter 6 identifies the stochastic convergence theorem used in its proof.

Important assumptions travel with the conclusions. In particular, the proofs for finite discounted MDPs are not automatically results for undiscounted, partially observed, continuous-state, or nonstationary problems.

The script checks the exact policy values, linear versus iterative evaluation, the v/q identity, positive affine reward transformation, and agreement among planning algorithms and exhaustive policy enumeration. Seeds, episode budgets, tolerances, and termination/truncation rules are stated in the corresponding chapters.

The recorded numbers are observations from a small controlled experiment. They help detect implementation mistakes and illustrate theory; they do not replace convergence proofs or establish performance on larger tasks.

## Experiments and self-checks

Each linked chapter ends with questions and answers. This index ties the ten-chapter path to concrete checks; the two scripts are standalone and share the same grid convention.

| Chapters | Evidence to reproduce | Self-check focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–3 | Exact values, Bellman residual, q/v identity, all eight deterministic policies | Markov state, matrix direction, optimality and reward assumptions |
| 4 | VI, PI, and truncated PI against the exact optimum | Evaluation versus improvement; stopping rules |
| 5–6 | MC seeds and three step-size schedules | Coverage, truncation, stochastic convergence conditions |
| 7 | Paired MC/TD prediction; four TD control algorithms across ten seeds | Target policy, bootstrapping, n-step terminal flushing |
| 8 | Least squares versus projected TD; unstable mean drift; five DQN seeds | Semi-gradient, training distribution, replay/target roles |
| 9 | Analytic gradients versus finite differences; 100 gradient batches | Objective, occupancy normalization, baseline validity |
| 10 | Three actor learners across 20 seeds; off-policy weighting identity; continuous quadratic critic | Critic bias, state/action corrections, action chain rule |

For Chapter 7, mean prediction RMSE at 100/1,000/5,000 episodes is 0.012451/0.003703/0.001730 for MC and 0.021242/0.007717/0.005969 for TD. The JSON reports sample standard deviations as well. All four control methods extract a greedy policy worth 0.9 in all ten seeds; the epsilon-0.2 behavior remains worth 81/91. These chosen budgets and steps are illustrative, not an algorithm ranking.

The later script verifies policy-gradient and neural-network derivatives with finite differences, distinguishes approximation error from sampling error, and compares actor values with exact model evaluations. Chapter 10 presents its learning figure and numerical summary. Terminal states have zero continuation; unexpectedly long episodes raise an error instead of being silently relabeled terminal. Chapters 7–10 report sample standard deviations with denominator N−1; the earlier script's cross-seed summaries use denominator N and retain their original values.
